Statistical Computing & Data Visualization¶

Python · Pandas · Matplotlib · College Scorecard · U.S. mortality data

Selected outputs from coursework in Modern Statistical Programming. This report highlights data visualization, descriptive analysis, dataset aggregation, and a reusable plotting function. Original implementations are not included in this public report. Figures and summary values are retained from the completed notebook.

1. Student Debt Across Institutions¶

The histograms compare the distribution of median student debt among public, private not-for-profit, and private for-profit institutions. Colors distinguish institution levels (four-year, two-year, and less-than-two-year). This helps illustrate how debt levels vary across institutional categories, beyond a single average.

2. Completion Rates Across Institutions¶

These distributions compare completion rates across the same institution categories and levels. The separated histograms show differences in the shape and concentration of completion rates rather than assuming one rate represents every institution.

Interpretation¶

The following observations are adapted from my original coursework write-up.

1f. 4-year universities have the highest median student debt on average compared to other levels. This may reflect the longer time spent in school for four-year institutions compared to two-year. Two-year institutions also have higher median student debt on average than less than two year institutions. Private institutions appear to have higher median student debts than public institutions at the four year and two year level, but private universities that are less than two years seem to have lower median student debt. The mean completion rate for private Not-for-profit institutions at the four year level appears to have a higher mean completion rate than public and private-for-profit instiutions. It seems that at the two year level the mean completion rate is much higher for private institutions than public ones. Institutions at the less than two year level seem to have the highest mean completion rate overall.

6. Mortality Data Visualization Function¶

I developed a function to display counts of firearm-related deaths by a selected intent and demographic variable. The examples below demonstrate how the same plotting approach can create different views without manually writing a new chart for each category.

Example calls from the coursework:

plot_gun_data("suicide", "age", policevar="")
plot_gun_data("homicide", "sex", policevar="")

Suicide deaths by age range, 2024¶

The first chart groups ages into decades and plots the number of recorded suicide firearm deaths within each age group. It displays counts, not age-specific rates. Differences between bars may reflect both population sizes and underlying mortality patterns.

Homicide deaths by sex, 2024¶

The second chart uses the same function to show homicide firearm-death counts by sex. Because these are raw counts, the chart alone does not establish differences in population-adjusted risk.
